# Unified Architectural Poster Design

Read this file completely whenever poster mode is selected. Poster mode consumes the original source and produces one final designed artifact; any draft or worksheet is internal only.

## Canonical contracts

Build a Poster Feature Card from observed source evidence and explicit user associations before writing copy.

Use only observed features or user-authorized associations in visible copy; never invent brands, achievements, identities, locations, or events.

Poster recomposition may crop, mask, or remove non-essential areas with diagonal cuts, stepped corners, polygonal apertures, or lineage-derived geometric frames, but it must preserve the selected subject anchors and must never stretch anatomy or architecture.

Typography, palette, geometric framing, and the selected architectural lineage must form one visual system.

Visible English copy defaults to one title of one to four words and one optional microline of no more than six words; verify every character exactly.

## Poster Feature Card

Record these fields before prompt compilation:

1. Source artifact type.
2. Architectural lineage evidence or shared-geometry rationale.
3. Directly observed subject features.
4. Explicit user-authorized associations, kept separate from observations.
5. One primary subject anchor.
6. Up to two secondary subject anchors.
7. Protected relationships, including pose, contact, occlusion, and object ownership.
8. Expendable areas that may be cropped, masked, simplified, or removed.
9. Exact visible English title and optional microline.
10. One primary layout geometry and at most one subordinate geometry.
11. Typography route.
12. Palette route.
13. Period Evidence Card, including `specific`, `broad`, or `insufficient` confidence and the fallback used.
14. Resolved rendered period typography construction from field 13: proportion, rhythm, stroke contrast, spacing, alignment, and ornament restraint.
15. Resolved rendered period palette from field 13: material-pigment source, core evidenced source colors, one subordinate off-route connector accent if used, and forbidden stereotype colors.

Fields 14–15 are the poster's resolved rendered selections from field 13, not duplicated second cards. If poster composition requires no specific override, inherit both directly from the Period Evidence Card; record any override without raising its evidence confidence.

Do not convert an inference into an observed fact. A helmet may support user-authorized associations such as riding, racing, or speed only when the user supplies those associations or visible evidence clearly establishes them.

## Historical-surreal Visual Thesis Card

For a routed historical poster, collage, editorial, zine-like, or stylized architectural transformation, use the historical-surreal grammar by default; when the user explicitly requests no surrealism or a restrained conventional poster or editorial, disable the impossible spatial gesture and monumental-fragment formula while retaining source-shape linkage, anti-cutout composition, integrated typography, period evidence, and source fidelity.

Complete the shared Visual Thesis Card in `historical-surreal-collage.md` and bind these poster decisions before prompt compilation. When the conventional override applies, record its disabled fields explicitly rather than silently restoring the surreal formula:

1. One source-derived proposition and one layout system.
2. The semantic minimum, one primary anchor, and only the supporting cues required for recognition.
3. On the default historical-surreal path, one monumental architectural fragment that acts on the source rather than completing a background behind it; on the conventional override, no required monumental fragment and no complete background behind a cutout.
4. No more than two or three source-faithful planes, with one asymmetric crop and controlled off-frame continuation.
5. At least two changed structural relationships—crop or scale, placement, overlap, figure-ground, repetition, or negative-space distribution—while source projection, topology, contacts, and protected anchors remain intact.
6. One dominant field, one structural secondary color, and at most one high-chroma accent, each linked to a source contour, axis, rhythm, material, or shadow.
7. An active negative-space mass that controls hierarchy, eye path, and a type interaction rather than serving as a leftover margin.
8. Typography that passes behind or through, sits beside, is partially occluded by, or aligns with source geometry.
9. Selective rendering and medium-native marks, with expendable edges allowed to simplify, disappear, or continue off-frame.
10. Explicit rejection of repeated corners or emblems, badge grids, frame stacks, wallpaper fills, detached caption/title/footer strips, and arbitrary decorative shapes.

The source, architectural fragment, color, and type must share a source-shape linkage. If removing a color field or graphic device does not weaken balance, movement, figure-ground, or meaning, redesign it or omit it. Never force a fixed ratio, desaturation, flat color, torn paper, diptych, or microtext merely because another collage used it.

## Copy system

- Use one uppercase English title of one to four words.
- Use zero or one microline of no more than six words.
- Quote every visible string verbatim in the generation prompt and prohibit all other letters, numbers, logos, signatures, and watermarks.
- Prefer concrete, image-derived phrases such as `RIDE THE LINE`, `BUILT FOR SPEED`, `STONE AND SHADOW`, or `AXIS OF LIGHT` only when their concepts are evidenced or user-authorized.
- Never invent a brand, team, event, year, place, award, identity, or product specification.
- At full-size QA, compare every generated character with the quoted copy. Missing, substituted, duplicated, or extra characters fail.

## Geometric recomposition

Choose one primary device: diagonal cut, stepped corner, polygonal aperture, arch aperture, lineage-derived grid, or monumental edge crop. At most one subordinate device may support it.

- A selected anchor may cross one mask boundary to create depth, but its identity-bearing silhouette must remain readable.
- Protect head/helmet shape, hand-to-object contact, essential architectural bays, and other selected relationships.
- Crop expendable background or lower-priority detail before cropping an anchor.
- Never stretch a person, prop, arch, column, bay, repeat cell, or typeface to fill a frame.
- Do not stack unrelated masks, decorative borders, or magazine-cover furniture.

## Typography and palette routing

Route period evidence, Latin typography construction, material-pigment palette, confidence, and fallback through [period-style-systems.md](period-style-systems.md). Use the most specific supported system; if only a lineage is supported, use its broad fallback, and if lineage evidence is insufficient, use the shared geometric fallback without inventing a historical label. An evidenced source material or pigment belongs to the core palette; only an off-route branding or reference color is limited to one subordinate connector accent.

Period structure informs legible Latin display lettering; it does not authorize script imitation. Never fabricate Arabic-looking Latin, fake Chinese strokes or characters, or invented exact non-Latin copy. Exact Arabic, Persian, Chinese, or other non-Latin copy must be supplied explicitly by the user, preserved verbatim, and verified before use.

## Integrated poster composition

Plan image and type together in the same generation prompt. Typography must occupy a geometrically reserved zone, align to the same axis or module as the architecture, and share the artwork's edge, pigment, print, and aging behavior. Do not generate a finished painting and then place clean digital text on top. The subject, architectural medium, geometric cuts, lettering, and palette must read as one authored poster.

For poster mode, section 2 must declare the crop/mask geometry and retained anchors, section 3 must declare typography and palette, and section 4 must quote every visible string verbatim.

## Poster QA

- Check the primary and secondary anchors against the original source at full size.
- Check protected contact and occlusion relationships.
- Check crop safety and confirm that no anatomy, object, architecture, or letterform is stretched.
- Check every visible character against the exact quoted copy.
- Check that lettering shares the selected medium rather than floating as a digital overlay.
- Check that palette, type category, geometric framing, architecture, and cultural lineage form one system.
- Verify rendered typography and palette against the recorded period evidence and confidence.
- Confirm that a `broad` or `insufficient` fallback has acquired no specific-period label, motif, or dynastic ornament.
- Distinguish core source colors identified as evidenced materials or pigments from the single optional off-route connector accent; reject stereotype palettes in either role.
- Reject results that resemble a background replacement, a cutout with a caption, or a crowded magazine cover.
