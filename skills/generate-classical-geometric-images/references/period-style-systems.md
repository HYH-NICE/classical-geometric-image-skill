# Evidence-Driven Period Style Systems

Use this reference whenever period, regional lineage, typography, or palette affects a generated artifact. Treat the matrices as construction routes for contemporary Latin display lettering and image materials, not as claims that a modern font existed in the named period.

## Canonical contracts

Build a Period Evidence Card from user direction and visible architectural, ornamental, material, manuscript, or painting evidence; never infer period from a person's appearance.

When specific-period evidence is weak, use a restrained broad-lineage system; when lineage evidence is insufficient, use shared classical geometry and do not invent a historical label.

Typography must derive proportion, rhythm, stroke contrast, spacing, alignment, and ornament restraint from the selected period while keeping Latin letters legible and never fabricating Arabic-looking or Chinese-looking glyphs.

Choose color from period materials and pigments rather than flags or generic ethnic color stereotypes; a source color may appear only as a restrained connector accent.

In that contract, `source color` means an off-route branding or reference color. If the Period Evidence Card identifies a visible color as an evidenced period material or pigment—such as vermilion timber, red sandstone, or İznik cobalt—it belongs to the core palette and may retain its historically appropriate primary role. Never promote a flag color or generic ethnic stereotype by calling it material evidence.

Record period evidence confidence as specific, broad, or insufficient, and state the fallback used.

## Historical-collage period binding

Bind every evidenced broad or specific route's period-native typography and pigments to the selected collage mechanics without inventing a period; use a source accent only when it is compatible with both the source and the selected period.

An explicit no-surreal override remains cultural: retain the evidenced broad or specific lineage, period-native typography and pigments, source-shape linkage, and integrated type after removing the impossible gesture and monumental fragment.

Route enamel, glazed ceramic, and gemstone-like accents by the selected cultural lineage, confine them to architectural or typography-linked nodes behind the subject, and keep their combined visual area approximately 5–12%.

### Restrained material-accent routing

Material accents are optional structural punctuation, never the base medium. Place them only at evidenced joints, voussoirs, tile intersections, bracket or roof nodes, plaque/type junctions, measured geometric vertices, or one architectural seam behind the protected subject. Keep the remaining approximately 88–95% in the selected ink, gouache, watercolor, mineral-pigment, engraving, fresco, manuscript, or other routed base medium.

| Route | Permitted restrained accents | Placement logic | Reject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Islamic | Faience, glazed tile, lapis, turquoise, malachite, restrained shell gold | Evidenced tile intersections, geometric vertices, portal or muqarnas joints, or typography-linked architectural seams | Gem scatter, generic gold sparkle, jewelry display, unsupported Mughal/Nasrid/Ottoman mixing |
| Chinese classical | Architectural liuli, celadon, cloisonne-like enamel, cinnabar, restrained gilded joints | Evidenced roof or bracket nodes, plaque/type junctions, measured bay accents, or mineral-pigment seams | Festival red-gold default, jewelry cloisonne objects, plastic glazed roofs, invented dynasty color |
| Western classical | Enamel inlay, colored glass, lapis, garnet, bronze, pietra-dura-like nodes | Evidenced order, entablature, vault, mosaic, plaque/type, or measured-section junctions | Black-gold luxury framing, jewel pedestal, indiscriminate stained glass, plastic polished stone |

If the Period Evidence Card does not support a lineage or material, omit the accent or use the shared base medium. Never infer a jewel or enamel route from the subject's clothing, helmet, product finish, or perceived identity.

## Period Evidence Card

Record these fields before routing typography or color:

1. Lineage.
2. Regional tradition.
3. Approximate period.
4. Evidence sources: explicit user direction and visible architecture, ornament, materials, manuscript, or painting.
5. Confidence: `specific`, `broad`, or `insufficient`.
6. Fallback used.
7. Prohibited neighboring periods or traditions.
8. Typography construction: proportion, rhythm, stroke contrast, spacing, alignment, and ornament restraint.
9. Palette and material source.
10. Connector accent, if any.
11. Explicit user-supplied exact non-Latin copy, if any.

Do not use clothing, facial features, skin tone, or a person's presumed identity as period evidence. Separate user direction from observed evidence and note contradictions rather than silently blending them.

## Routing decision

1. If the user specifies a period, use it unless reliable visible evidence directly contradicts it; when contradictory, state the conflict and use the user's direction only after clarification or frame it as an intentional reinterpretation.
2. Record `specific` confidence only when the user explicitly supplies the period, a dated or provenanced reference establishes it, or at least two independent period-diagnostic cues from different evidence families converge, such as structure plus ornament, material plus manuscript technique, or architecture plus a provenanced painting. A single common or compatible cue can never yield `specific` confidence. Conflicting cues force `broad` or `insufficient` confidence.
3. If evidence establishes a lineage but not a period, select its broad fallback and record `broad` confidence.
4. If lineage evidence is insufficient, select shared geometric fallback, record `insufficient` confidence, and do not attach a historical label.
5. Retain at most one off-route source color as a subordinate connector accent; it must occupy less visual emphasis than the material-pigment palette. An evidenced material or pigment is instead part of the core palette and is not demoted merely because it is visible in the source.

`Compatible` cues can support lineage but do not distinguish a period. `Diagnostic` cues contribute to `specific` confidence only under the convergence rule above. Rows marked broad are fallbacks and can never receive `specific` confidence.

## Islamic systems

Coverage: Andalusi/Nasrid and Persianate Timurid/Safavid are kept discoverable as families, but their broad and specific prescriptions remain separate.

| Route | Evidence cues | Latin typography structure | Material-pigment palette | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Andalusi regional (broad only) | Compatible: horseshoe or polylobed arches, carved stucco, timber ceilings, and glazed tile establish an Iberian Islamic context without a dynasty | Narrow-to-medium capitals; measured arch or tile-grid alignment; crisp strokes and sparse terminal ornament | Limewashed ivory, warm stucco, cedar brown, cobalt, copper green, muted turquoise | Applying a Nasrid label, Ottoman tulips, generalized desert gold, pseudo-Arabic Latin |
| Nasrid (specific) | Diagnostic convergence: dense sebka and epigraphic stucco zones with prismatic muqarnas, glazed dadoes, or secure Alhambra provenance | Slender capitals; interlaced baseline modules; tight repeated rhythm; ornament remains outside the letter skeleton | Pale carved stucco, cedar, cobalt, copper green, muted turquoise, small red-earth accents | Treating any horseshoe arch as Nasrid, Mughal pietra dura, fake calligraphic glyphs |
| Mamluk (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: ablaq monumental portals plus stone muqarnas, geometric woodwork, or inlaid metalwork | Tall compact capitals; strong vertical rhythm; alternating dark-light alignment; angular joins without script mimicry | Limestone cream, basalt charcoal, walnut, brass, copper, restrained cobalt glass | Ottoman rococo, Persian manuscript delicacy, flag palettes, invented Arabic forms |
| Ottoman (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: central dome and half-dome hierarchy or pencil minarets plus İznik floral tilework or provenanced court material | Broad calm capitals; generous spacing; dome-axis centering; limited floral punctuation outside letter skeletons | Marble white, İznik cobalt, turquoise, bole red, sage green, walnut, dull gold | Nasrid sebka as default, Mughal jewel saturation, fake calligraphic swashes |
| Timurid (specific) | Diagnostic convergence: tall portal iwans and high ribbed turquoise domes with mosaic faience, banna'i brick-tile pattern, or provenanced manuscript illumination | Tall refined capitals; elongated horizontal rhythm; monumental panel alignment; restrained illuminated terminals | Lapis, turquoise, cobalt, plaster white, ochre, malachite, restrained shell gold | Safavid haft-rangi as default, Ottoman dome shorthand, invented Perso-Arabic glyphs |
| Safavid (specific) | Diagnostic convergence: bulbous dome and Isfahan-style axial ensemble with haft-rangi floral tile, muqarnas portal, or secure Safavid provenance | Refined high-contrast capitals; wider panel rhythm; controlled floral punctuation outside letters | Cobalt, turquoise, plaster white, yellow ochre, malachite green, black, restrained gold | Calling any turquoise dome Safavid, Timurid ribbing by default, neon cyan |
| Mughal (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: cusped arches and chhatris with red-sandstone/white-marble contrast, pietra dura, or charbagh axial evidence | Stately medium-wide capitals; symmetrical spacing; fine-to-heavy stroke contrast; axial or framed-panel alignment | Red sandstone, Makrana-like marble white, black stone, jade green, lapis, carnelian accents | Ottoman tile dominance, Persian route without South Asian evidence, jewel-tone overload |
| Broad Islamic fallback (broad only) | Compatible: repeated geometric grids, arches, tile, or carved ornament support lineage but not a period | Legible geometric or condensed Latin capitals; modular spacing; alignment to evidenced arch, star, or polygon rhythm; minimal ornament | Warm plaster, stone, timber, charcoal, one evidenced mineral blue or green | Naming a dynasty, mixing regional signatures, fake Arabic-looking Latin, invented Arabic copy |

These routes influence Latin display construction only. Exact Arabic or Persian copy must be supplied by the user, preserved verbatim, and verified by a competent reader or authoritative source; never synthesize plausible-looking text.

## Chinese classical systems

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Song (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: restrained timber/bracket proportions or ruled book structure with monochrome ink technique, celadon, or dated provenance | Compact high-contrast serif or disciplined sans; clear horizontal rules; balanced counters; quiet spacing and minimal terminals | Ink black, paper buff, celadon gray-green, weathered timber, gray tile, mineral azurite in small areas | Qing polychrome excess, generic imperial red-gold, brush-shaped fake Chinese strokes |
| Yuan (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: robust monumental timber planning with provenanced court manuscript, ceramic, or cross-regional material evidence | Broad sturdy capitals; decisive verticals; ample counters; axial spacing with restrained contrast | Ink, warm paper, timber brown, gray tile, cobalt on porcelain white, muted mineral green | Treating blue-and-white alone as proof, Ming vermilion default, pseudo-characters |
| Ming (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: formal standardized bays with securely associated glazed-roof, garden-lattice, or blue-and-white material evidence | Upright medium-wide capitals; modular bay rhythm; crisp serif or sans construction; controlled contrast | Vermilion timber, yellow or gray glazed tile only when evidenced, ink, porcelain white, cobalt, stone gray | Qing famille-rose density, indiscriminate gold, flag red, faux calligraphy |
| Qing (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: dense painted bracket work with provenanced enamel, famille-rose, imperial roof, or court ornament evidence | Formal high-contrast capitals; framed panel alignment; tighter ornamental cadence kept outside letter skeletons | Mineral blue-green, vermilion, ochre yellow when evidenced, ink, stone, restrained gilding, famille-rose accents | Applying imperial colors to vernacular scenes, maximal ornament everywhere, invented Chinese text |
| Broad Chinese-classical fallback (broad only) | Compatible: timber bay rhythm, tiled roofs, garden, or manuscript structure establishes lineage without a reliable dynasty | Legible inscriptional serif or disciplined condensed sans; ruled-line or bay alignment; square proportions without imitating character strokes | Ink black, paper ivory, timber brown, gray tile, one evidenced mineral blue-green or restrained cinnabar | Dynasty labels, automatic red-gold palette, fake Chinese strokes or characters, invented exact copy |

These routes do not turn Latin letters into Chinese calligraphy. Exact Chinese copy must be supplied by the user, preserved verbatim, and verified by a competent reader or authoritative source; do not invent characters, radicals, or translated wording.

## Western systems

Coverage: Ancient Greek and Roman prescriptions remain separate; Greco-Roman is only a broad umbrella. Baroque and Neoclassical prescriptions likewise remain separate within Baroque/Neoclassical coverage.

| Route | Evidence cues | Latin typography structure | Material-pigment palette | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ancient Greek (specific) | Diagnostic convergence: trabeated temple form and Greek order details with painted architectural or securely provenanced ceramic evidence | Monumental capitals with open spacing, low ornament, and alignment to stylobate, bay, or frieze rhythm | Pentelic-like marble, limestone, terracotta, red and yellow ochre, carbon black, restrained Egyptian blue | Roman arches or inscription structure, pristine white-only antiquity, Renaissance scrollwork |
| Roman (specific) | Diagnostic convergence: concrete arch, vault, dome, engaged orders, or Roman inscription evidence with mosaic, fresco, or secure provenance | Roman-capital proportions; flared terminals; measured inscription spacing; alignment to bays or panels | Travertine, marble, brick red, Pompeian red, yellow ochre, carbon black, bronze | Treating every classical order as Roman, Greek temple purity, Beaux-Arts density |
| Greco-Roman umbrella (broad only) | Compatible: orders, pediments, entablatures, ashlar, mosaics, or carved inscriptions establish classical antiquity but not Greek versus Roman | Restrained monumental capitals; measured spacing; alignment to bay, frieze, or inscription panel | Marble or limestone, terracotta, ochres, carbon black, bronze | Greek or Roman label, later classical ornament, pristine white-only antiquity |
| Medieval manuscript (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: ruled parchment and period letter/initial construction with historiation, marginal technique, or dated provenance | Legible Latin display capitals with compact manuscript rhythm; strong stroke contrast; enlarged initial separated from body copy | Parchment, carbon ink, iron-gall brown-black, vermilion, azurite, malachite, restrained gold leaf | Fake illegible blackletter, Renaissance symmetry, false exact-font claims |
| Renaissance (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: measured perspective and proportional façade with humanist manuscript, early print, rustication, or dated provenance | Humanist Roman capitals or oldstyle structure; moderate contrast; generous rational spacing; axial and perspective-grid alignment | Warm limestone, plaster ivory, terracotta, umber, verdigris, ultramarine used sparingly | Baroque swash excess, medieval compression, modern luxury serif clichés |
| Baroque (specific) | Diagnostic convergence: curved or dynamically layered fronts, broken pediments, and theatrical axes with period sculptural or painted evidence | High-contrast capitals or serif structure; compressed-expanding rhythm; controlled flourish outside core letterforms | Warm stone, umber, deep red, patinated bronze, restrained gilding | Neoclassical austerity, generic black-and-gold luxury, uncontrolled swashes |
| Neoclassical (specific) | Diagnostic convergence: planar archaeological orders, severe symmetry, and restrained ornament with dated measured-drawing or material evidence | Crisp high-contrast serif structure; severe axial spacing; clean terminals; low ornament | Pale stone, plaster ivory, graphite, bronze, muted blue-green, restrained burgundy | Baroque curves and theatrical layering, Beaux-Arts eclectic density, luxury-brand cliché |
| Beaux-Arts (specific route) | Diagnostic convergence: monumental symmetry and layered orders with grand stair, sculptural program, or iron-and-glass civic evidence | Formal wide capitals or high-contrast display serif; hierarchical scale; strong centerline and plaque alignment | Limestone, marble, patinated bronze, slate, burgundy, muted ultramarine, restrained gilding | Calling any columned façade Beaux-Arts, Art Deco geometry without evidence, ornamental crowding |
| Broad Western-classical fallback (broad only) | Compatible: orders, proportional bays, manuscript ruling, or measured axes establish lineage but not a period | Legible Roman-derived capitals or restrained serif; align to column bay, cornice, page rule, or measured axis; minimal ornament | Stone, ivory, sepia, graphite, bronze, one evidenced earth or mineral accent | Invented period labels, mixing medieval and classical motifs, false exact-font claims |

Typography labels in this table describe proportional and structural behavior, not guaranteed historical typeface identities. Use an exact typeface name only when it is independently verified and actually available.

## Shared geometric fallback

| Route | Evidence cues | Latin typography structure | Material-pigment palette | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shared geometric fallback (insufficient only) | Compatible geometry supports symmetry, axes, modular grids, arches, polygons, or measured construction but no reliable lineage | Neutral Roman-derived or geometric capitals; open counters; moderate contrast; spacing and alignment follow the observed grid; no culture-coded terminals | Source-native stone, paper, timber, metal, graphite, and earth neutrals; at most two structural colors plus one subordinate connector accent | Historical labels, dynasty or regional motifs, flags, ethnic color shorthand, pseudo-Arabic or pseudo-Chinese forms |

## Non-Latin copy boundary

Display lettering structure is not historical script simulation. Never fabricate Arabic-looking Latin, fake Chinese strokes or characters, or any plausible-looking non-Latin copy. Use exact Arabic, Persian, Chinese, or other non-Latin text only when the user supplies the exact string; preserve it verbatim, verify it with a competent reader or authoritative source, and omit it when verification is unavailable.
