# Historical Surreal Collage

Use this grammar to turn source evidence and an evidenced classical lineage into one designed image. Preserve roughly 60–70% historical and lineage discipline while allowing roughly 30–40% collage tension; this is a creative guide, not a pixel quota.

## Canonical contracts

For a routed historical poster, collage, editorial, zine-like, or stylized architectural transformation, use the historical-surreal grammar by default; when the user explicitly requests no surrealism or a restrained conventional poster or editorial, disable the impossible spatial gesture and monumental-fragment formula while retaining source-shape linkage, anti-cutout composition, integrated typography, period evidence, and source fidelity.

On the default historical-surreal path, apply the following thesis and route contracts.

Choose exactly one source-evidenced visual thesis and one memorable spatial gesture; every fragment, crop, type relationship, color accent, and effect must support them.

Apply the Islamic-fragment contract only when the Period Evidence Card selects Islamic lineage at specific or broad confidence; never apply it to Chinese, Western, shared-geometric, or insufficient-evidence routes.

Render the source and one monumental Islamic architectural fragment as one unified historical-surreal collage; never place a complete background behind a cutout subject or add detached caption bars.

Use two or three source-faithful planes at most, one dominant field, one structural secondary color, and at most one high-chroma accent; reject repeated corner emblems, symmetric badge grids, frame stacks, wallpaper motifs, and arbitrary floating shapes.

At thumbnail size require one focal hierarchy, one governing movement, and recognizable source anchors; at full size require intentional crop edges, overlaps, textures, typography, and cultural detail.

Apply the Chinese-route contracts only when the Period Evidence Card selects Chinese lineage at specific or broad confidence; never apply them to Islamic, Western, shared-geometric, or insufficient-evidence routes.

Express the shared collage grammar through evidenced bay rhythm, dougong, jiehua or ruled-line construction, stele or plaque proportion, woodblock, album-leaf, or mineral-pigment behavior without fake Chinese strokes.

Preserve the source topology, measured relationships, and semantic minimum while allowing at most two or three source-faithful planes.

Apply the Western-route contracts only when the Period Evidence Card selects Western lineage at specific or broad confidence; never apply them to Islamic, Chinese, shared-geometric, or insufficient-evidence routes.

Express the shared collage grammar through evidenced orders, entablature, vault, measured section, engraving plate, fresco, manuscript, or architectural-capriccio behavior without generic luxury-ad framing.

Make typography participate through alignment, partial occlusion, negative space, shared geometry, or the governing motion axis.

When period evidence is broad and lineage is Islamic, Chinese, or Western, use the corresponding broad-lineage system; only insufficient lineage evidence uses shared classical geometry.

Render the complete protected subject and every protected held object above optional coating, dissolution, abrasion, particles, glaze, enamel, and gemstone effects; those effects may continue only through adjacent background or genuine negative openings.

A unified base medium still applies to subject and architecture; subject protection never authorizes a photographic cutout.

## Protected foreground and layer order

Build the unified illustration in this visual order: substrate; optional background coating, material accents, and dissolution; incomplete architecture and integrated typography; protected subject and protected held objects; grounding and contact shadow. This is a compositional occlusion order inside one generated artwork, not permission to create or paste separate raster cutouts.

Derive the protected foreground from the current Source Fidelity Card. For a person or object source, protect the complete identity-bearing silhouette and every visible helmet or headwear surface, face when visible, clothing, anatomy, hands, shoes, held object, functional joint, subject-object contact, foot-ground contact, and source-specific projection or proportions. Architecture and type may disappear behind this foreground and reappear beyond it. They may pass through only a genuine negative opening already present in the source silhouette; they must not stain, glaze, sparkle, abrade, or dissolve across the protected foreground.

The subject and architecture must still share the selected substrate, line, pigment, edge, shadow, and detail hierarchy. Use occlusion and edge discipline to protect the foreground, never a photographic halo, hard extraction fringe, mismatched sharpness, or unchanged photo detail.

## Visual Thesis Card

Complete this card after source and period evidence are resolved and before prompt compilation:

1. **Source evidence:** visible geometry, material, color, gesture, and lineage cues; keep observations separate from user-authorized associations.
2. **Semantic minimum:** the smallest set of forms, contacts, measured relationships, and exact characters that still identifies this source.
3. **Primary anchor and supporting cues:** choose one remembered primary subject or anchor. Add no more than two subordinate cues, and use none unless each is necessary to preserve recognition or an essential relation. Do not impose a minimum relationship budget beyond the primary anchor and essential relations.
4. **Visual thesis:** state one source-evidenced proposition in one sentence.
5. **Single spatial gesture:** name one memorable or impossible change of scale, continuation, displacement, or figure-ground logic. It must derive from the source or evidenced lineage; never introduce a second surreal giant or competing contradiction.
6. **Monumental fragment:** select one architectural fragment that acts on the source. It is not a complete architectural background.
7. **Governing axis:** identify the source horizon, bay rhythm, gaze, motion, projection line, or measured axis that organizes crop, fragment, type, and eye path.
8. **Plane budget:** assign no more than two or three source-faithful planes and state what each retains, merges, omits, transforms, or exposes.
9. **Type interaction:** specify how type aligns with, passes behind or through, is partially occluded by, or continues the source geometry without becoming a detached caption.
10. **Active negative space:** reserve a quiet mass that shapes the focal hierarchy, type interaction, and eye path rather than functioning as leftover margin.
11. **Color structure:** choose one dominant field, one structural secondary color, and at most one high-chroma accent. Record the source shape and compositional job of each color.
12. **Protected anchors:** list identity-bearing silhouettes, faces, contacts, object ownership, topology, structural bearings, measured lines, and exact characters that no crop or overlap may damage.
13. **Expendable edges:** list peripheral areas that may crop off-frame, dissolve selectively, merge into the field, or remain unrendered.
14. **Forbidden template furniture:** name the repeated corners, badges, frame stacks, wallpaper fill, detached title/footer strips, arbitrary floating shapes, or other stock devices to exclude.
15. **Protected effect mask:** list the complete subject and held-object surfaces, genuine negative openings, background continuation paths, and contact-shadow edges that govern every optional effect.
16. **Material accent budget:** record the selected lineage vocabulary, architectural or typography-linked nodes, approximate combined visual area, and forbidden luxury or plastic treatment.

Use source-shape extraction as the common skeleton for image fragments, color fields, and typography. Translate requests for a named artist into medium, era, mark, hierarchy, and spatial traits; do not imitate or copy a named artist or an existing artwork.

When the conventional override is active, complete the evidence, semantic minimum, anchor, axis, plane, type, negative-space, color, protection, and anti-template fields, but record the impossible spatial gesture and monumental fragment as `disabled — explicit conventional request`. Preserve integrated source and architecture through source-shape continuation, crop, overlap, material, and type rather than a background-and-cutout composite.

## Fragment and field budget

- On the default historical-surreal path, build one proposition with one layout system; the primary anchor and monumental fragment must read as one event, not foreground pasted over scenery. On the conventional override, build the proposition from source-shape continuation, crop, overlap, material, and type instead.
- Keep only the semantic minimum. Use the abstraction decisions **retain, merge, omit, transform, expose** to create selective incompleteness and large quiet masses.
- Use one resolved focal region and let supporting regions simplify, stop at an open edge, or breathe. Medium-native marks must describe material behavior rather than act as a uniform filter.
- Change at least two structural relationships from the source—crop or scale, placement, overlap, figure-ground, repetition, or negative-space distribution—while preserving projection, contacts, topology, and protected anchors.
- On the default historical-surreal path, let the monumental fragment continue asymmetrically off-frame and never close it into a complete facade, room, skyline, or ornamental backdrop. On the conventional override, use asymmetric source-derived architecture without requiring a monumental fragment.
- Every fragment and graphic device must be traceable to a source contour, axis, rhythm, shadow, material, or evidenced architectural system. Remove arbitrary decoration.

## Typography interaction

Typography belongs to the collage structure. Derive its proportion and rhythm from the selected period system, then attach it to a source edge, governing axis, opening, bay, shadow, or movement. It may pass behind, through, or beside the anchor, share an occlusion, or occupy active negative space, but it must preserve exact copy and protected geometry.

Do not force microtext or a detached caption strip. Do not fabricate Arabic-looking Latin, fake Chinese strokes, or invented non-Latin characters. Typography may be omitted when the selected mode or user request requires no visible copy.

## Route-specific translation

Apply these monumental-fragment translations only on the default historical-surreal path. Under the conventional override, retain the selected lineage's evidenced geometry, material, medium, and type system without the impossible gesture or monumental-fragment formula.

- **Islamic lineage:** choose one evidenced monumental fragment such as an iwan edge, muqarnas zone, arch, vault, courtyard plane, measured geometric screen, or manuscript-derived field. Use its actual axis, subdivision, material, and light behavior; do not convert it into a complete palace or generic exotic backdrop.
- **Chinese classical lineage:** use evidenced timber-bay rhythm, roof or bracket relationships, ruled construction, plaque proportion, print behavior, or mineral-pigment planes. Preserve the source topology and measured contacts; never use ornamental pseudo-characters as texture.
- **Western classical lineage:** use evidenced order, entablature, vault, section, engraving, fresco, manuscript, or capriccio mechanisms. Preserve load-bearing and measured relationships; reject centered luxury-product framing, gold-on-black default styling, and detached prestige copy.
- **Broad lineage evidence:** use the corresponding broad Islamic, Chinese, or Western lineage system and omit unsupported period-specific claims or ornament.
- **Insufficient lineage evidence:** use shared classical geometry and the recorded insufficient-evidence fallback. Do not invent a dynasty, style label, ornament, or cultural route.

## Anti-template failures

Reject the result when it contains a complete background behind a cutout, a centered unchanged subject, a second surreal contradiction, equally weighted fragments, repeated corner devices, symmetric badge grids, stacked frames, wallpaper fill, detached header/footer bars, arbitrary dots or collage scraps, uniform texture, or color that can be removed without changing balance, eye path, figure-ground, or meaning.

Do not universally force desaturation, flat color, torn paper, diptych layout, 3:4, 3:5, 9:16, or microtext. Select ratio, color behavior, edge treatment, typography, and medium from the current source, route, and user request.

## Thumbnail and full-size tests

At thumbnail size, verify three-second recognition of the primary anchor, one dominant focal hierarchy, one governing movement, and one legible proposition. On the default historical-surreal path require one impossible spatial gesture only; on the conventional override require none. Merge or remove any supporting cue that competes with the anchor or turns quiet space into filler.

At full size, compare the artifact with the source and Visual Thesis Card. Verify semantic minimum, projection, topology, contacts, protected anchors, exact characters, intentional off-frame crops, plausible overlaps, medium-native textures, source-linked color, typography interaction, and evidenced cultural detail. Mentally remove the structural secondary color and accent; if balance, eye path, figure-ground, and meaning do not weaken, redesign their shapes or omit them.
