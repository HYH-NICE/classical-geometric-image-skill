# Historical Dissolution

Read this file completely whenever it is selected. Historical dissolution is optional: apply it only when requested or appropriate, and omit it when the Source Feature Map exposes no safe expendable edge.

## Canonical placement contract

Historical dissolution defaults to approximately 6–10% of one trailing expendable edge and follows motion, wind, or the governing geometric axis.

If no safe expendable edge exists, omit dissolution rather than damage an identity anchor, contact relationship, functional joint, structural bearing, measured line, repeat boundary, or exact visible character.

Route dissolution from the current source class and selected medium; drawings use graphite loss, blueprint grain, erased construction lines, or paper abrasion rather than smoke, debris, or volumetric particles.

Declare the one dissolution edge, its direction, approximate percentage, and every protected anchor before generation.

When dissolution is selected, distribute low-density loss along one consistent trailing side from top to bottom rather than concentrating it in one localized patch; skip protected segments and continue through adjacent atmosphere.

When dissolution is selected for a protected subject, route it as a background wake in one consistent top-to-bottom direction and never erode protected subject pixels.

Interpret the selected medium as the selected final/output unified medium. The selected final/output unified medium controls dissolution/material-loss behavior. Source class and source medium supply visible evidence, anchors, geometry, and safe-edge candidates only; they must never override the final/output medium.

Treat the selected effect as one full-height directional field on one trailing side/system, not as several unrelated edges. For a protected person, object, vehicle, or prop, the field is a background wake: establish it above the subject, continue beside and behind the subject, bridge the complete protected silhouette through adjacent background or atmosphere, and continue below the subject without touching protected pixels. Use intermittent, low-density traces at safe background portions from the upper extent to the lower extent; do not erode every pixel, form a continuous destructive cut, remove chunks, or create a uniform dense band. Protected gaps do not reverse or restart the field. Keep total perceived loss across the artifact at approximately 6–10%. Omit the effect when no safe distributed background field exists.

## Source-class routing

Classify the current source before placing any loss:

- **Person or group:** Protect the complete visible person, including helmet or headwear, face when visible, anatomy, clothing, hands, limbs, shoes, gaze, pose, inter-person contact, held-object contact, and ground contact. Place the entire wake in adjacent background or atmosphere from top to bottom; never use a helmet, shoulder, garment, butt, hand, held object, leg, or foot as an erosion surface.
- **Object or product:** Protect the complete recognizable object and its profile, branding or exact visible characters, controls, functional joints, supports, and contact with people or ground. Carry one low-density trailing-side wake through adjacent background from above to below, bridging the entire object rather than using its casing or edge as a loss surface.
- **Vehicle or prop:** Protect the complete recognizable vehicle or prop, controls, functional joints, wheels or supports, carried loads, and contact with people or ground. Carry the same trailing direction through adjacent background from above to below, bridging the entire vehicle, prop, occupant, wheel, joint, and contact.
- **Architecture:** Protect topology, openings, roofline, primary axes, structural bearings, spans, joints, thresholds, circulation, and ground connection. Distribute sparse traces down one nonbearing trailing side and its adjacent atmosphere, bridging openings, bearings, inscriptions, and thresholds without changing direction.
- **Landscape, interior, or exterior:** Protect the defining horizon, terrain or waterline, circulation and thresholds, primary vegetation silhouette when identity-bearing, and every architectural structure. Carry one directional field through non-defining upper atmosphere, safe edge foliage or peripheral elements, and lower atmosphere or ground fringe; bridge every protected horizon, structure, threshold, and primary silhouette.
- **Drawing, pattern, or manuscript:** Protect measured lines, construction relationships, repeat boundaries, exact visible characters, diagram legibility, and all required marks. Distribute drawing-native loss along one side of the page or field from top to bottom, bridging protected geometry, tiling, annotations, and required marks through blank paper or background.
- **Mixed source:** Combine every applicable protected zone, select one shared safe edge only, and omit dissolution if the classes do not expose a common expendable direction.

Place loss on exactly one trailing side/system. Default to approximately 6–10% total perceived loss; exceed 10% only with explicit user authorization. Derive one consistent direction from observed or requested motion first, wind second, and the governing geometric axis otherwise. Never invent motion or wind merely to justify the effect.

## Medium-first routing

Select the final/output unified medium before any optional lineage specialization. That output medium controls the physical behavior of the effect:

- **Photography:** Use source-matched print or emulsion grain, edge exposure, or edge abrasion. Add physically plausible material dust only when the pictured material and context support it; never add generic smoke or overlay particles.
- **Painting or substrate:** Use pigment granulation, paper or canvas fiber, or fresco, plaster, or stone powder appropriate to the selected medium and substrate.
- **Dimensional render:** Use material-specific fragments or dust derived from the depicted object surface while keeping lighting and perspective unchanged, or use restrained opacity and edge breakup consistent with the render material; never introduce a VFX cloud.
- **Drawing or sheet:** Use graphite loss, blueprint grain, erased construction lines, or paper abrasion; never substitute smoke, debris, or volumetric particles.

For example, a photo transformed into a watercolor or ink poster uses pigment, paper, fiber, or ink behavior rather than emulsion abrasion. A photo transformed into a photoreal or aged print may use the photographic route because photography remains the selected final/output medium.

## Optional lineage specialization

Only after selecting the medium, specialize its pigment or material vocabulary when the lineage and period are evidenced:

- **Islamic specialization:** Within the selected medium, use evidenced mineral-pigment wear, plaster powder, stone granulation, pigment flake, or glaze loss appropriate to its substrate.
- **Chinese specialization:** Within the selected medium, use evidenced ink feathering or dry-brush loss, paper-fiber abrasion, mineral-pigment dust, or restrained surface dust appropriate to the work.
- **Western specialization:** Within the selected medium, use evidenced fresco powdering, stone grain, charcoal loss, plate wear, or parchment abrasion appropriate to the period and substrate.

Keep the effect native to the unified medium and current palette. Exclude generic gold sparks, cinematic smoke, four-sided loss, halos, unrelated colors, damaged anchors, digitally overlaid particle fields, localized butt or shirt-hem patches, dense horizontal scratch bands, inconsistent directions, uniform erosion, continuous cuts, and missing upper-to-lower distribution. The result must remain readable at full size and subordinate at thumbnail size.
