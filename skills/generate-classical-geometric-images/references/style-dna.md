# Classical Geometric Style DNA

Use this reference to make spatial, lineage, material, and camera choices before prompt construction. Treat geometry as the organizing system, not as surface decoration.

## Shared visual grammar

- **Axes and centers:** Establish one dominant organizing axis, center, or deliberately balanced pair before adding detail. Relate any subordinate axes explicitly through alignment, hierarchy, or symmetry. Align openings, subjects, paving, ceilings, and distant focal elements to this system. Avoid unrelated competing centers or symmetry that breaks at major structural elements.
- **Modules, repetition, and rhythm:** Choose a believable primary bay, tile, panel, or span module; repeat it with controlled variation. Relate smaller or larger subordinate modules by an explicit multiple, subdivision, or boundary rule. Let the modular system set column spacing, opening widths, floor joints, and ornament scale. Avoid random spacing, copy-paste sameness, or patterns that ignore corners and edges.
- **Thresholds and framed depth:** Compose depth as nested transitions—door, arcade, court, hall, screen, or window—with clear foreground, middle ground, and destination. Use occlusion and tonal falloff to make each layer readable. Avoid flat scenic backdrops and portals that lead nowhere.
- **Structural legibility:** Make loads and spans plausible: supports meet beams, arches spring from supports, roofs have credible bearing, and ornament follows construction. Avoid floating masses, unsupported openings, decorative joints, and details that contradict the structural system.
- **People as scale anchors:** Use people sparingly to reveal bay width, step height, ceiling scale, and depth. Keep them naturally placed and subordinate to the spatial order; do not enlarge or shrink people to force monumentality.
- **Authentic aged craft and material:** Show restrained wear where hands, weather, water, and time would act: softened edges, varied glaze, limewash mottling, timber checking, bronze patina, and repaired joints. Preserve craft irregularity without turning the building into a ruin or covering it with generic grime.

Geometry must control the image even when construction guides are invisible: axis, proportion, module, repetition, hierarchy, and nested depth should remain evident in the finished composition.

## Source Fidelity Card and composition routing

For a photo, architecture, or person edit, record a **Source Fidelity Card** before selecting style. Identity anchors mean only visible, task-relevant, non-inferential features explicitly needed to preserve the requested subject; never infer ethnicity, nationality, religion, health, or other sensitive traits.

The Architectural Source Card is the architecture-specific block of the Source Fidelity Card. Record silhouette/topology; dominant axes; horizon/camera height; primary and secondary vanishing structure; symmetry; visible bay/column/window rhythm and counts; roofline, openings, overlaps, scale, defining ornament, materials, light direction, clutter, and the **semantic minimum** that makes the source recognizable.

Classify evidence as **Hard locks** (identity, topology, required viewpoint, function, and spatial relationships), **Soft preferences** (grading, atmosphere, and unlocked minor crop/placement), or **Expendable detail** (non-identity-bearing clutter). Never classify requested items or safety/functional features as expendable.

Choose one composition system: one primary composition strategy may use compatible subordinate descriptors, but they must not compete with its hierarchy. For a photo, architecture, or person scene, choose exactly one primary strategy from source evidence:

| System | Selection evidence | Failure guard |
|---|---|---|
| Axial/frontal | Strong symmetry, centered approach, frontal façade | Keep the governing center and matched bilateral rhythm; do not fabricate symmetry. |
| Oblique depth | Diagonal façade, receding arcade, dominant vanishing direction | Preserve horizon, depth order, and convergences; do not flatten the view. |
| Vertical procession | Tower, stair, stacked registers, strong upward reading | Retain base-to-crown continuity and believable vertical scale; avoid stretched structure. |
| Courtyard enclosure | Perimeter wings, court center, nested thresholds | Preserve enclosure, circulation, and wing relationships; do not open the court into a generic vista. |
| Monumental crop | Source depends on close mass, edge pressure, or partial façade | Keep scale cues and defining cropped edges; do not invent a complete elevation. |
| Detail study | Identity resides in a joint, opening, ornament, or material assembly | Keep attachment, substrate, and local scale legible; do not turn the detail into detached decoration. |

Optionally add compatible subordinate descriptors for view direction, spatial archetype, or crop scale; these refine the primary strategy and never establish a second axis or hierarchy. For a pattern record `N/A — flat orthographic tile`; for a manuscript record `N/A — drawing-sheet projection` instead of forcing a scene strategy.

Derive geometry from the source's actual axes, modules, counts, and projection. Do not impose decorative golden-ratio overlays.

Use a retain/merge/omit/transform budget: **retain** the semantic minimum and identity-bearing geometry; **merge** only redundant, non-defining detail; **omit** only expendable clutter; **transform** only user-authorized style or structure while honoring hard locks. Repeated architectural bays, columns, and openings—including their count and spacing—are identity-bearing unless the user permits redesign.

## Architectural tradition families

Treat Islamic, Chinese, and Western architecture as broad tradition families, not essentialized pure kits. Before adding detailed elements, select an evidenced regional and period subtradition from the user request, explicit reference, or architectural or ornamental evidence. The elements below are alternatives and compatible vocabulary to select from, never an all-at-once checklist. When the evidence does not support a subtradition, stay with shared classical geometry and omit invented lineage detail. Keep families separate unless the user explicitly requests fusion.

| Tradition family | Positive structure, space, decor, and material choices | Negative guardrails |
|---|---|---|
| **Islamic** | According to the evidenced subtradition, organize a courtyard around calibrated axes and layered framing, and select pointed arches, domes, or muqarnas only where appropriate. Give muqarnas a credible substrate, attachment method, and transition geometry; it may articulate or finish a transition and need not be load-bearing. Derive selected star grids or arabesque from a consistent geometric field. Use zellij only with regional evidence—it is regionally specific, not a universal Islamic finish. Favor evidenced combinations of plaster, stone, glazed tile, carved wood, and metalwork with local seams and craft variation. | Avoid unrelated classical orders, culturally unrelated roof profiles, arbitrary ornament, star patterns that do not resolve at boundaries, unsupported muqarnas, or stacking courtyard, pointed arch, dome, muqarnas, star grid, zellij, and arabesque as a generic checklist. |
| **Chinese** | According to the evidenced subtradition, organize an axial courtyard sequence with a clear bay module, horizontal extension, roof rhythm, and alternating solid/void thresholds. Use the appropriate dougong hierarchy to express rank and frame logic; align timber frames, screens, paving, and roof bays. Favor evidenced combinations of timber, tile, stone plinths, plaster, bronze, and paper with credible joints and weathering. | Avoid implausibly oversized or weightless roofs, decorative-only structural joints, random bracket sets, incoherent bay spacing, vertical monumentality that defeats the horizontal system, or combining every listed element irrespective of building type and period. |
| **Western** | According to the evidenced subtradition, establish orders and bays consistently, then select pediments, domes, rose windows, or centrality only where period and building type support them. Coordinate facade divisions, openings, stairs, and interior volumes with documented proportions or simple rational proportions appropriate to that period and type. Favor evidenced combinations of cut stone, plaster, marble, timber, bronze, and glass with readable masonry courses and assemblies. | Avoid inconsistent orders, unsupported monumentality, columns that bear nothing, pediments without a compositional role, and prestige symbols detached from type. Do not combine classical orders or pediments with Gothic rose windows absent evidence of a historical transition or an explicitly requested fusion. |

### Controlled fusion

Fuse only on explicit user request. First establish a shared geometry—dominant axis, primary module, proportion, and depth sequence. Then assign each tradition a distinct structural or spatial role rather than mixing every surface: for example, one governs the load-bearing frame while another governs screens or courtyard geometry. At every junction, resolve dimensional compatibility, fastening or support, weathering exposure, and the transition between materials. Limit the material palette and repeated motifs. Do not create a symbol collage or unsolicited cross-family fusion.

Select cultural lineage only from the user request, an explicit reference, or architectural or ornamental evidence. Never select it from a person's perceived ethnicity, nationality, religion, facial features, name, clothing alone, skin tone, or other sensitive traits.

## Automatic space matching

| Source framing | Match to space | Fallback lens cue, full-frame equivalent | Composition and controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close portrait | Niche, window, or shallow arcade | 35–50 mm | Use framed or local symmetry and low ornament density; keep the face dominant and background depth shallow. Maintain enough camera distance to avoid facial projection distortion, cropping rather than moving excessively close. |
| Full body | Axial courtyard, colonnade, or steps | 28–35 mm | Preserve the source viewpoint; establish one dominant vanishing structure with geometrically consistent secondary points, a readable ground module, and human-scale steps, rails, openings, and bays. |
| Group | Open courtyard, hall, or long gallery | 24–35 mm | Let architectural rhythm organize the grouping; use a clear center or balanced composition and enough lateral space for natural spacing. At or near 24 mm, keep people away from distortion-prone frame edges. |
| Existing architecture or ordinary photo | A corresponding space and structure from the same evidenced lineage; if none is evidenced, preserve building type and function and use shared classical geometry | Optional reconstruction default: 24–35 mm with a restrained tilt-shift feel | Preserve source viewpoint, field of view, function, and main massing. Strengthen axes, proportion, repetition, and layered thresholds without replacing the building type; never let the reconstruction default override source perspective. |

All lens ranges are full-frame-equivalent fallback cues, used only after matching the source field of view and perspective. Keep camera height consistent with the source or a believable standing/eye-level viewpoint. Use horizon, convergence, floor joints, and opening scale as focal-length cues. Keep verticals straight unless the source deliberately uses perspective convergence; control wide angles so near edges do not balloon. Identity and body proportions always outrank a default focal range. For portrait edits, never stretch faces, heads, limbs, or body proportions to fit the architecture—adjust crop, camera distance, lens choice, and space instead.

## Architectural photography and materials

- Render stone as mineral and weight-bearing, plaster as softly varied and matte, glazed tile as selectively reflective, timber as directional-grained, bronze as subtly oxidized, and paper as fibrous and light-transmitting. Add material-specific seams, joints, courses, fasteners, and edge wear at believable scales.
- Match light response to material: broad soft highlights on plaster and worn stone, crisp but limited reflections on glaze and bronze, anisotropic grain response on timber, and gentle subsurface transmission through paper. Avoid one uniform plastic sheen.
- Use neutral white balance, broad dynamic range, restrained saturation, straight verticals, a controlled wide angle, and a clean composition. Preserve highlight detail and open shadows without flattening depth.
- Build monumentality from scale, axis, depth, hierarchy, and measured repetition—not movie fog, teal-orange grading, god rays, oversharpening, exaggerated HDR, or theatrical haze.

## Construction visibility

Hide guide lines, grids, compass marks, and scaffolding geometry in ordinary photos, architectural photography, and portrait edits. In geometric-pattern or classical-manuscript modes, show precise compass arcs, regulating lines, or grids only when the requested artifact benefits from visible construction; keep them exact, intentional, and subordinate to the finished geometry.

## Selection checklist

Before prompt building, confirm:

- [ ] Source mode, viewpoint, camera height, subject scale, and invariants are identified.
- [ ] Cultural lineage is evidenced by the request or reference, or the design stays with shared geometry.
- [ ] One dominant organizing system and one primary module govern the scene; every subordinate axis or module is explicitly related to them.
- [ ] Space type matches the source viewpoint, field of view, and perspective; the full-frame-equivalent fallback focal range does not distort identity or body proportions.
- [ ] Structure carries believable loads; ornament follows its geometry and boundaries.
- [ ] Materials have a limited palette, credible joints, material-specific light response, and restrained aging.
- [ ] People establish scale without becoming compositional clutter.
- [ ] Construction guides are hidden or shown according to output mode.
- [ ] The evidenced regional/period subtradition supports every detailed element; vocabulary is selected, not treated as an all-at-once checklist.
- [ ] Fusion, if any, is explicit, role-based, geometrically unified, technically resolved at junctions, and not a symbol collage.
